## Teaching Big Data and Cancer Biology — Through a High School Lens
<p>This summer I volunteered as a teaching assistant for Dr. Wang’s “AI and BigData for Cancer Biology” program, the course met once a week for a month where high school students dove into the intersection of coding and cancer research. It was easily one of the most rewarding things I’ve done all year.</p>

<p>The goal of the program was simple: show students that the same Python code they use to make a game or a calculator can also be used to explore real biological data. We mixed in bits of upper-level cancer biology whenever it was needed — just enough to make the coding meaningful.</p>

<p>Most mornings started with short lessons on Python: reading data, cleaning it up, making graphs, and running simple statistical tests. In the afternoons, we tied those skills back to real problems. One of the highlights was a group project where students used the Genotype-Tissue Expression (GTEx) dataset to look at integrin gene expression across different human organs. They ran ANOVA and t-tests, made plots, and learned how to ask, “What does this actually tell us?”</p>

<p>By the end of the program, each student designed a small independent research project — something personal that connected coding to cancer biology. Some explored gene expression patterns, others looked at signaling pathways, but all learned how data can tell a biological story.</p>

<p>What stood out to me most was their curiosity. Once they realized they could actually use coding to explore biology, they took off running. I mostly played the role of guide — helping debug code, answer biology questions, and celebrate the little victories along the way.</p>

<p>It was inspiring to see high school students engage with complex ideas so fearlessly. Programs like this remind me why I love both teaching and research — because curiosity, when given the right tools, grows fast.</p>
