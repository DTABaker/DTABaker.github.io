How to use your attempts

Complete each answer field and select Check my answer. A valid attempt unlocks the solution, including when your answer needs correction. Reveal one step at a time, revise your answer, or open the full worked solution. New problem generates fresh practice in the selected topic, or selects another topic when mixed review is active; Another like this stays with the current topic. Mixed review gives extra weight to unpracticed topics and topics whose latest recorded first attempt was incorrect.

Calculated quantities allow 1% relative rounding tolerance. Lewis electron and lone-pair counts require the exact integer count. Aim for three significant figures in calculated quantities and carry extra digits through intermediate steps; significant figures are not graded separately. Scientific notation such as 2.46e-3 works. A tiny nonzero answer is not accepted as zero. The units beside a field are the required units; enter the number alone.

Progress records the first valid attempt on each displayed problem. Rechecking that problem does not add another attempt. These counts are study feedback, not secure grades or a validated measure of mastery. Worked steps come from explicit equations and authored explanation templates. The tool does not interpret arbitrary written reasoning.

Read the model before calculating

Freezing: the tool uses water, Kf = 1.86 °C·kg/mol, and a pure-water freezing point of 0.00 °C. The depression ΔTf is a positive magnitude; subtract it from the pure-solvent temperature. Nonelectrolytes have ideal i = 1. Electrolyte exercises assume the stated ideal dissociation; real particle effects can deviate from that approximation. Read about colligative properties in OpenStax.

Solubility: every exercise assumes a saturated solution at equilibrium with excess solid at a fixed temperature. Generated solubilities and Ksp values are hypothetical teaching data, not literature measurements for the named salts. The model treats ions as ideal and neglects secondary equilibria. The thermodynamic constant is dimensionless: concentrations are normalized by c° = 1 mol/L. The familiar classroom shorthand uses the numerical concentrations in M. Read about saturation, ion products, and common ions.

In the Ag₂CO₃ model, carbonate protonation/hydrolysis and silver complex formation are neglected, so free [CO₃²⁻] is set equal to s. The same general neglect of side reactions also applies to the other solubility exercises. Those assumptions matter when relating the algebra to real aqueous chemistry. Read about coupled equilibria.

Titration: measured endpoints are treated as equivalence points, using balanced-reaction mole ratios. The requested molarity is the original sample concentration. Sulfuric-acid problems specify full neutralization of both protons. Acetic-acid equivalence stoichiometry does not imply that its equivalence-point pH is 7. The tool does not calculate titration curves or pH. Read about titration stoichiometry.

Molecular drawings: draw the molecule on paper and compare it with the solution. The tool grades structured checkpoints, not your sketch. Its finite bank contains H₂O, HCl, NH₃, HBr, CH₃OH, and CH₃CH₂MgBr; repeated molecules are expected. Partial charges describe bond polarity and differ from formal charges. For C–Mg, C is δ− and Mg is δ+. The Grignard picture is a simplified covalent bookkeeping representation, not a complete account of aggregation or solvation. Read about the electron-rich carbon of Grignard reagents.

For more background, review OpenStax's solubility rules and balanced chemical reactions.

Your work stays in your browser

No student account, API key, or external generation service is needed. This app sends no answers or scratchpad working to a server and includes no analytics. It saves only per-topic practice counts and the most recent recorded first-attempt outcome in this browser. It does not save your answer text, scratchpad, name, or student identifier. Reset clears those local practice records; if browser storage is unavailable, counts last only for the current session. The hosting provider may keep ordinary website request logs.

A copied problem link contains a topic, generator version, and random seed. With the same generator version and unchanged code, that link reproduces the same problem. It includes no student answers or working. New problems draw from bounded teaching data, so values or molecular variants can repeat.
